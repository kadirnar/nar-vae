# 2025–2026 TTS research review and architecture decision

This document records the evidence used for NAR-VAE's diffusion, multilingual-text, and
reference-voice design. It deliberately separates a paper's claims from claims established for
this repository. NAR-VAE has no released trained checkpoint yet, so quality, speaker similarity,
WER/CER, latency, and throughput remain evaluation targets rather than achieved results.

## Non-negotiable constraints

- DACVAE is an immutable codec dependency. Acoustic-model changes happen on its continuous
  latents or in conditioning modules, never inside `nar_vae/dacvae`.
- New acoustic checkpoints use a strict variance-preserving diffusion objective, not rectified
  flow presented under a broad "diffusion" label.
- The multilingual text backbone is frozen and external to the acoustic checkpoint. Training
  consumes cached contextual states and learns only a small projection adapter.
- Reference audio must support unseen speakers. Speaker IDs are pairing metadata, never closed-set
  model inputs.
- Low-cost settings are hypotheses until compared against the quality baseline on held-out data.

The 2025 flow systems remain useful architectural references, but they cannot determine the new
loss or sampler because the strict objective requirement rules out straight rectified-flow paths.

## Frozen multilingual conditioner

The canonical provider is
[XPhoneBERT](https://huggingface.co/vinai/xphonebert-base), whose
[official repository](https://github.com/VinAIResearch/XPhoneBERT) describes a BERT-base phoneme
model pretrained on 330M phoneme-level sentences from nearly 100 languages/locales. It predates
the review window, but it is a reusable component rather than evidence for the acoustic design.

The exact provider contract is pinned in the training YAML:

- encoder and tokenizer Hub IDs plus full commit revisions;
- config, model, and tokenizer filenames plus SHA-256 digests;
- selected hidden layer, feature width and cache dtype;
- provider-token alignment policy and cache version.

The official input is a word-segmented phoneme sequence. The provider therefore requires supplied,
reviewed phonemes in canonical mode, uses the tokenizer's native BOS/EOS behavior, and rejects
unknown speech phones or unsupported style/control semantics. It never maps invented controls to
arbitrary carrier phones. Raw orthography is not silently presented as multilingual phonetic
support; production G2P and normalization must be pinned and evaluated per advertised language.

The frozen 88M-class backbone runs during dataset preparation and once per inference request. It
is not registered in the acoustic model, is absent from acoustic checkpoints, receives no
gradients, and is not repeated at every diffusion step. Cached states feed a trainable linear
adapter whose parameter cost is included in the acoustic-model count.

## Selected acoustic objective

Time follows the generation direction: `t=0` is Gaussian noise and `t=1` is clean DACVAE latent.
For a clean sample `x`, Gaussian noise `epsilon`, and cosine angle `phi=pi*t/2`:

```text
alpha(t) = sin(phi)
sigma(t) = cos(phi)
x_t      = alpha(t) * x + sigma(t) * epsilon
v_target = sigma(t) * x - alpha(t) * epsilon
```

The implementation also supports a checkpointed positive log-SNR schedule shift. The shifted
coefficients are renormalized so `alpha^2 + sigma^2 = 1`, and generic probability-flow ODE solvers
apply the exact time-varying `d phi_shifted / dt` chain factor. Deterministic DDIM uses the stable
v-prediction reconstruction:

```text
x_hat       = alpha * x_t + sigma * v
epsilon_hat = sigma * x_t - alpha * v
x_next      = alpha_next * x_hat + sigma_next * epsilon_hat
```

Objective name and schedule shift are atomic checkpoint and manifest metadata. A legacy checkpoint
without objective metadata remains explicitly rectified flow. Loaders reject mismatched or partial
metadata rather than guessing.

## Selected voice-cloning path

Reference audio remains in immutable DACVAE latent space. Training selects another utterance from
the same speaker, requires different utterance identity/audio hash, and uses deterministic bounded
crops. The bidirectional reference encoder processes timbre and temporal cues. A learned-query
resampler then compresses arbitrary reference length to eight fixed summary tokens in the
canonical configuration.

The fixed bottleneck is motivated by both cost and leakage control:

- cross-attention cost no longer grows with reference duration after encoding;
- every CFG branch has one versioned, fixed speaker-token topology;
- the learned queries can aggregate complementary voice/prosody evidence instead of mean pooling.

It does not prove transcript leakage has been removed. Evaluation must include same-text versus
different-text references, cross-language references, shuffled references, short/long references,
and content-recovery probes. Multi-reference training should sample distinct segments/utterances
rather than duplicate one recording.

## Cost choices

- `small` is the canonical acoustic preset; `nano` and `tiny` are legitimate lower-cost ablations.
- Target latent patching is outside DACVAE. `P2` is the default efficiency candidate, with `P1`
  as the quality control and `P4` as an aggressive ablation.
- DDIM profiles use 32, 16, and 8 neural evaluations. Only 32 is the initial quality profile;
  all three require measurement on trained weights.
- Frozen text states and DACVAE latents are cached during data preparation.
- Exact MAS, duration prediction, BF16, activation checkpointing, cost-based batching, and DDP
  remain available. None changes DACVAE.
- Step caching, branch skipping, distillation, and post-hoc latent normalization are not part of
  the canonical VP path until separately validated.

## Required ablations and release gates

At minimum, compare:

1. `nano`, `tiny`, and `small` acoustic presets;
2. target patch sizes 1, 2, and 4;
3. 4, 8, and 16 speaker summary tokens plus the legacy uncompressed path;
4. DDIM at 8, 16, and 32 evaluations and a high-accuracy ODE reference;
5. frozen hidden layers and cache dtypes;
6. single-language, multilingual, code-switch, and cross-language reference conditions;
7. short, long, same-text, different-text, mismatched-style, and multi-utterance references;
8. every declared target/reference-language pair separately, including intelligibility and
   speaker similarity rather than only a pooled multilingual mean.

Report trainable and resident parameter counts separately, plus GPU-hours, peak memory,
throughput, real-time factor, WER/CER, speaker verification similarity, duration error, repetition
and truncation rates, perceptual metrics, and blinded listening results. No configuration name
(`quality`, `fast`, or otherwise) substitutes for these measurements.
